\begin{abstract}
Which quantities do collisions preserve, and how do they determine macroscopic
transport? We study these questions in two four-wave resonance models.
For the one-dimensional nearest-neighbour pinned chain, with any fixed coupling
$0<d<1/2$, we prove that every almost-everywhere finite measurable collision
invariant for the dispersion $\omega_d(x)=\sqrt{1-2d\cos x}$ has the form
$A+B\omega_d$, where $A,B$ are constants. Local resonance averages first give a
smooth representative; periodic compatibility then determines the invariant.
At critical resonances, the energy mismatch and the full collision difference
share a vanishing factor. Retaining this cancellation yields a densely defined
closed collision form, while the invariant classification and compact resonance
averages give a positive spectral gap.

For the wave kinetic equation with quadratic dispersion and a sharp cutoff
to a momentum cube in three dimensions, we prove a strong-collision limit from
arbitrary smooth positive local Rayleigh--Jeans initial data, throughout any
prescribed finite interval on which the corresponding five-moment Euler solution
remains smooth and positive. If $c$ is the collision strength, the distribution
and macroscopic parameter errors are respectively $O(c^{-3/4})$ and $O(c^{-1})$
in the norms specified below. The collision frequency vanishes at the corners,
where the solution retains the free-transport trace of its initial data.
Actual five-moment matching gives an exact decomposition of relative entropy
into microscopic and macroscopic errors, controlled by weighted dissipation
and the flux equations. The microscopic entropy balance further yields strong
convergence of the resonance current and a first-order transport law for all
five moments. The Onsager tensor is symmetric and nonnegative, with a
seven-dimensional kernel in the fifteen-dimensional gradient space. Its eight
dissipative directions are the traceless symmetric part of the momentum-parameter
gradient and the energy-parameter gradient.
\end{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords.} Four-wave resonance; collision invariants; pinned chain;
Rayleigh--Jeans distribution; Euler limit; Onsager tensor; first-order transport.
